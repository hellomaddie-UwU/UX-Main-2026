Context

WoofPay was a passion project initiated by my boss at Webpuppies, a Singaporean IT company. The product vision was two-fold:


WoofPay

An affordable alternative to Shopify that helped small business owners with taxes, payments, and a social-media-embeddable online store; and


WoofEvents

A form-driven social media post generator designed for entrepreneurs without a marketing team. This was considered an early, pre-AI version of what generative tools now do for content creation.



With no existing brand, no prior design system, and a one-month window, a design system needed to be built from scratch while product screens were being designed simultaneously. As the sole UX/UI Designer at Webpuppies, the design system was both a deliverable and a personal necessity.

Impact

The design system was built to serve a lean team moving fast. It’s success was measured by how scalable and easily adaptable it was for the Dev Team.

The Design System's Outcome
Result 1: Development Proceeded Without Interruption

Richard worked from the design system for two months after handoff without returning with questions or requests. Richard was known to reach out when something was unclear, so his continued silence throughout development is interpreted as a signal that the system was coherent and complete enough to build from independently.

Result 2: Consistent Visual Foundation Across Two Products

By establishing the design system before development began in earnest, both WoofPay Payments and WoofEvents were built from the same visual foundation(same tokens, components, and interaction patterns); rather than diverging as separate implementations.

Result 3: Scope Was Clean and Held

No components were requested retroactively and no gaps were identified post-handoff. The scope was delivered as planned and submission happened upfront between the dev team and the product owner.

Features
FEATURE 1
Brand-New Visual Identity System

WoofPay had no existing brand when the project began. Style guide options were presented to the boss upfront. His selection became the locked creative direction for the entire project and all future outputs. This approach prevented mid-project pivots and gave me a stable visual reference from day one.

FEATURE 3
Tax Filling Flow on WoofPay

I created a custom flow built on top of the basic components I made was informed by my direct research into Xero’s tax filing structure and Paymaya’s overall user journey flows. The complexity of Xero’s American-oriented tax system pushed me to apply a simplified tax flow that’s accessible and easy to use for all types of users(beginners to seasoned business owners).

image caption
FEATURE 4
Social Media Post Generator Flow on WoofEvents

A custom Typeform-inspired form flow that outputs a ready-to-edit social media post is the core product experience of WoofEvents. The editing interface was intentionally simplified per my boss's direction: no lasso tools or Photoshop-adjacent features; just a text editor and basic shape controls.

The rationale was reducing cognitive load for small business owners who weren't designers.

Research & Discovery
DISCOVERY 1
WoofPay’s Competitors

Maya’s web app was studied in full, its structure, purpose, functionality, and interaction patterns. Ecwid was researched as the primary benchmark for affordable Shopify alternatives. While for Xero, I made an account to independently study tax filing workflows firsthand(a self-initiated step beyond the project brief).

The Xero deep-dive revealed that American tax filing structures are significantly more complex than the simpler formats used in other markets. This directly informed the decision to simplify WoofPay's Tax Filing flow for a broader, more accessible audience.

RESEARCH
User Persona

Both products targeted small business owners and entrepreneurs seeking affordable alternatives to build their business, specifically those who couldn't justify the cost of Shopify, a physical store, or a marketing team. The target audience was designed to be as inclusive and market-agnostic as possible.

This was before Shopify launched their Lite Plan*

Key Insights
My Insight

Richard’s request to stop adding components was feedback that the system was legible. A design system that communicates clearly enough for a developer to continue without the designer present is more valuable than an exhaustive one that still requires constant explanation.

My Insight

WoofPay and WoofEvents serve the same user persona but in entirely different contexts(financial management vs. content creation). The design system needed to feel cohesive across both without flattening the distinct experience each product required.

My Insight

A Figma file served the immediate handoff need. With current tooling and vibe-coding capabilities, an internal component website would have served the developer better. The website would have possessed copy-pasting live components faster than hand-coding from a Figma reference. The constraint was the platform and the timeline, not the approach.

Design Iterations

The initial concept for WoofEvents included a full-featured editing environment that’s closer to a design tool like Canva. The boss requested a significant simplification of removing complex tools; retain only a text editor and basic shape controls. The rationale was explicit, reduce cognitive load for non-designer users, making the tool feel you’re editing, not designing. This pivot clarified the product's identity and made the interaction model more coherent for its target audience.

After the basic components were completed, Richard indicated he had enough context to continue independently and didn't need further component documentation. Rather than continuing to build components for their own sake, the scope was closed. Overachieving on a clear instruction is not a virtue in professional work. The right call was to respect the boundary and move on.

Reflection

Locking the visual direction early through the style guide selection process gave the entire project a stable foundation; no mid-project pivots, no stakeholder-driven rework, no additional headaches. Working in parallel with Richard meant the developer had something real to build from almost immediately, rather than waiting for a sequential design-then-develop process. The competitor research grounded the product decisions in real user experience rather than assumptions.

The design system was scoped for the two web apps only — marketing outputs (website, emailers, collaterals) were deferred to a future phase. Figma's token/variable system was not yet available at the time of the project; design tokens would have made the system more maintainable and scalable had the platform supported them.

I’d build a component website instead of a Figma file, which was the same conclusion reached on the MSPbot Welcome Screens Guideline and Design System Project. For a developer working in code, a live component library with copy-paste functionality is faster and more practical than navigating a Figma file. The one-month timeline and platform limitations were the real constraints at the time, not the approach itself.

I'd also apply Figma's token and variable system from the start. This feature that didn't exist yet when this project was built, but one that would have made cross-product consistency easier to maintain and update as the brand evolved.
