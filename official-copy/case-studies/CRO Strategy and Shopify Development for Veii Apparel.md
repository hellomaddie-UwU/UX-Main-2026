# CRO Strategy and Shopify Development for Veii Apparel

**Category:**
- Industry: Activewear and E-Commerce
- Output Type: Website

---

## Expected Deliverables

1. Redesigned product page template, featuring a size chart brought above the fold for faster sizing decisions (shipped)
2. Updated catalogue page template with a persistent CTA banner and collection dropdown navigation (shipped)
3. Updated desktop navigation (shipped)
4. On-site reward and loyalty system (shipped)
5. Women's collection landing page (in progress at contract end; not launched)

---

## ✌️ Outcome

Two of four shipped initiatives produced measurable conversion lifts. The size chart prominence update and CTA banner together moved conversion rate up by approximately 0.3 percentage points. The reward system produced the largest single result of the project: a roughly 1 percentage point CR increase and a 10% revenue lift within the measurement window.

Desktop navigation and catalogue dropdown changes produced no measurable CR change. The women's collection landing page was not completed before the contract ended due to the client's funding constraints.

> **Note:** Supporting reports and tracking files were not retained after the contract ended. Figures above are recalled from memory rather than formally documented.

---

## Context

Veii Apparel is a community-driven activewear brand selling performance hoodies, sweatshirts, and joggers through a Shopify storefront, with the bulk of its traffic driven by TikTok. In 2024, the founder brought in a CRO Strategist and Shopify Front-End Developer to improve store conversion, with Microsoft Clarity already installed and generating behavioral data. The engagement ran for approximately three to four months, starting in summer: a structurally difficult window for a catalog built almost entirely around cold-weather gear, and a constraint that shaped every expectation and outcome that followed.

---

## Impact

Five initiatives were scoped, built, and measured across the engagement. Each was pushed live to the production store after founder review of the staging environment, then tracked over a two-week window.

### Result 1: Product Page Redesign and CTA Banner (+0.3 pp CR lift, recalled)

Clarity data showed the size chart was buried below the fold, meaning users arrived at the add-to-cart point without having resolved their primary sizing question. Bringing the chart forward and adding a CTA banner to catalogue page templates produced a combined 0.3 percentage point lift in conversion rate across the two-week measurement window.

### Result 2: Reward and Loyalty System (+1 pp CR lift, +10% revenue, recalled)

A loyalty system was collaboratively scoped to target repeat purchase behavior rather than new visitor acquisition. Under summer conditions with a cold-weather catalog, retention had more room to move than acquisition. The result was the highest-impact outcome of the project: approximately 1 percentage point CR increase and a 10% revenue lift within the measurement window.

### Result 3: Navigation Updates (no measurable impact)

The desktop navigation update and catalogue dropdown were both shipped but produced no measurable change in conversion rate. TikTok-referred traffic typically lands directly on product pages rather than navigating from the top of the site, which likely limited the reach of these changes. The desktop navigation update reflected stakeholder preference rather than a behavioral data signal.

### Result 4: Women's Collection Landing Page (not launched)

A dedicated landing page for an upcoming women's collection was scoped and partially built after the founder recognised the front-end development range on offer. It was in progress when the contract ended due to funding constraints on the client side.

## Features

**Size Chart Prominence**
Moved the size chart above the fold on the product page template so visitors could resolve sizing questions before reaching the CTA, rather than after. Addressed the rage-click and drop-off pattern concentrated on product pages in the Clarity data.

**CTA Banner on Catalogue Pages**
Added a persistent call-to-action banner to catalogue page templates to create a clearer next step for users browsing without converting. Responded to the Clarity finding of low exploration depth across the catalogue.

**Collection Dropdown Navigation**
Surfaced collection categories as a dropdown on catalogue pages to reduce the clicks required to reach a relevant product. Intended to shorten the path between browse behavior and product page arrival.

**Reward and Loyalty System**
Built and launched an on-site reward system designed to incentivise repeat purchases from existing buyers. Collaboratively scoped with the founder as a retention strategy, not an acquisition one.

**Women's Collection Landing Page**
Designed and partially developed a dedicated campaign landing page for an upcoming women's collection. Extended the original CRO brief into campaign execution after the founder identified the front-end capability within the engagement.

---

## Research and Discovery

Microsoft Clarity was the sole research tool used throughout the project, installed by the founder before the engagement began. Session recordings and heatmap data surfaced three consistent behavioral patterns that shaped the prioritization of work:

**Mid-page drop-off:** The majority of visitors were leaving between the 50 and 75 percent scroll depth, suggesting friction or a loss of intent before reaching the CTA.

**Rage clicks on product pages:** Concentrated rage-click activity pointed to user frustration, most likely caused by sizing information being unavailable at the moment users needed it to commit to a purchase.

**Low catalogue exploration:** Visitors were not navigating between collections or exploring catalog depth. Most appeared to land and leave without browsing further, indicating that the catalog structure was not drawing users deeper into the funnel.

No formal user interviews or surveys were conducted. All design decisions were grounded in Clarity behavioral data and informed by a Udemy CRO course provided by the founder, which supported applying CRO methodology correctly to the findings.

---

## Key Insights

**Sizing friction was the most actionable conversion barrier**
Because the size chart was below the fold, users arrived at the CTA without having resolved their primary pre-purchase question. Moving sizing information earlier in the scroll removed a hesitation point at the moment of highest purchase intent, and the CR data supported it.

**Retention outperformed acquisition under seasonal constraints**
Because the product catalog was cold-weather dominant and the traffic window was summer, new-visitor acquisition had a structural ceiling that UX adjustments could not overcome. Targeting existing buyers through the reward system had more leverage, and the results reflected that.

**Navigation changes have limited impact when traffic bypasses navigation**
Because TikTok-referred users typically land directly on product pages, top-level navigation changes had a limited opportunity to affect conversion behavior. The desktop navigation update was a stakeholder preference call rather than a data-driven one; the two-week results confirmed that it did not move the needle.

---

## Design Iterations

Each change followed a staging-first workflow: build in staging, review and approve with the founder, document the change, push live, then measure over a two-week window before prioritising the next initiative. This made the process methodical even when the contract was short and the measurement windows were limited.

The scope of the engagement expanded mid-contract when the founder realised the role included front-end Shopify development capability. The original brief was behavioral data analysis and friction-point fixes. By the end, the work also included a loyalty system and a campaign landing page, both of which went beyond the original CRO mandate.

The loyalty system was a genuine collaborative addition: both parties identified that incentivising repeat purchases was a more realistic lever than pushing cold-weather apparel on summer traffic. The women's collection landing page was client-driven scope expansion and remained incomplete when funding ran out.

---

## Reflection

The clearest lesson from this project is about expectation-setting, not execution. No amount of UX improvement was going to overcome the structural reality of selling cold-weather gear in summer to impulse-driven TikTok traffic. That ceiling was always there. The mistake was not naming it explicitly at the start and documenting it as a constraint rather than carrying it as a personal frustration.

The concrete lesson: CRO work should be framed to stakeholders in terms of directional signals and realistic measurement timelines, not short-term lifts. A 0.3 percentage point improvement in conversion rate is a meaningful signal when understood correctly. Presented to a founder expecting summer sales to spike, it reads as underperformance. That framing gap is a communication problem, and it belongs on the strategist to resolve early.

If revisiting this project, the first client conversation would include a clear position: bad data is still good data, behavioral change takes time to surface in metrics, and the role is to remove friction between intent and purchase, not to manufacture conversion overnight. Taking that stance earlier would have protected both the quality of the work and the working relationship.
