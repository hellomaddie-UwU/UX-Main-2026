Context

Vyepti (eptinezumab-jjmr) is a pharmaceutical brand by Lundbeck, an IV migraine prevention medication for adults. Their appointment booking app was commissioned to solve a real operational problem in where nurses and secretaries were managing IV treatment schedules manually. Previously they relied on paper forms and paper patient records, with no standardized process for assigning treatment programs to patients.

The brief, as submitted in Vyepti's onboarding form, outlined 7 functional flows designed to automate bookings, digitize patient records, and provide staff with clear SOPs. Direct access to the end users(nurses and hospital secretaries) were unfortunately not part of the engagement.

Impact

The project produced two complete design directions under a single 3-week timeline. The gap between them is the case study.

The Design System's Outcome
Result 1: Proposed Direction Fulfilled the Brief

The original proposed screens covered all 7 flows from the onboarding form: Patient Registry, Patient Catalogue, Program Registration, IV Registration, Schedule Booking, User Profile, and KB Documentation. Each flow was designed to directly address one of the three stated pain points: manual booking, paper records, and no SOP for program assignment.

Result 2: Client Direction Reduced Functional Scope

The client replaced the proposed direction with her own designs. Of the 7 original flows, only 3 were retained: Program Registration, IV Registration, and User Profile. Ironically, Patient Registry, Patient Catalogue, Schedule Booking, and KB Documentation were the flows most directly solving the stated operational problems. An “About Vyepti” screen was added when duplicated content was already available on vyepti.com.

Result 3: Brand Consistency Was Not Maintained in the Client Direction

The client’s designs also deviated from Vyepti's own brand kit, this was the same brand kit that was provided to the me and was used as the visual foundation for the proposed direction. The deviation was left in the final file.

Accountability for internal brand consistency rests with the client*
Result 4: Presentation Culture Lesson Learned

The project surfaced a real professional lesson: Singaporean clients expect polished, finished screen presentations before giving approval, and not behind-the-scenes or work-in-progress views. This changed how I do client-facing presentations moving forward.

Observed pattern based on professional experience*
